1. Executive Overview & Governance Purpose
ISO 9001:2015 Compliant

This Quality Assurance and Quality Control (QA/QC) Governance Framework establishes the organizational structure, authority hierarchy, operational inspection protocols, and reporting mechanisms required across all project phases. The objective is to deliver built assets that strictly conform to approved designs, specifications, industry standards (ISO 9001:2015, ASTM, ACI, BS/EN), and statutory safety codes while minimizing rework and lifecycle operating costs.

2. Core Operational Separation: Quality Assurance (QA) vs. Quality Control (QC)

A fundamental requirement of construction quality governance is maintaining a clear operational separation between Quality Assurance (preventative processes) and Quality Control (execution inspection).

Governance Dimension Quality Assurance (QA) — Process Focus Quality Control (QC) — Product Focus
Primary Objective Prevent defects through systematic processes, planning, and pre-mobilization audits. Identify and rectify physical defects during execution and component assembly.
Operational Focus System-level: Policies, SOPs, ITP templates, qualification of vendors and batching plants. Execution-level: On-site inspections, slump/cylinder laboratory testing, punch listing.
Timing & Posture Proactive: Pre-mobilization, procurement, method statement and shop drawing review. Active / Reactive: Real-time during fabrication, concrete pours, and installation.
Accountability Corporate QA Director, Project QA Manager, Independent QMS Auditor. Discipline QC Engineers (Civil/Struct/MEP), Site Inspectors, Subcontractor QC Leads.
Primary Deliverables Project Quality Plan (PQP), Audit Reports, Risk Registers, Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). Inspection & Test Plans (ITPs), Test Logs, Non-Conformance Reports (NCRs), As-Built Verification.
3. Governance Hierarchy & Reporting Independence
QA/QC Manager
Defines corporate standards, conducts third-party surveillance, and arbitrates complex quality deviations across project packages.
QA/QC Superintendent
Author of the Project Quality Plan (PQP), oversees independent testing laboratories, chairs weekly quality reviews, and exercises stop-work enforcement.
QC Engineers (Civil, Struct, MEP)
Conduct daily inspections, witness field testing, enforce ITP criteria, log non-conformances, and verify hold points.
Site Construction Team (PM / Superintendents)
Responsible for production execution strictly within approved method statements and engineering tolerances.

Unconditional Stop-Work Authority

To ensure unbiased quality enforcement, site-level QA/QC personnel maintain an independent direct reporting line to Corporate Quality Leadership. QA/QC inspectors possess full authority to halt any work activity that compromises structural integrity, safety, or contract specifications without fear of production reprisal.

4. Non-Conformance Management (NCR) & Root Cause Analysis (RCA) Lifecycle
1

Identification & Tagging

Immediate physical red-tagging, isolation of defective work/materials, and formal NCR logging within 12 hours of discovery.

2

Root Cause Analysis

Multi-disciplinary assessment utilizing 5-Whys or Fishbone diagrams to pinpoint root systemic cause (operator, material, design, machine).

3

Disposition Category

Formally categorized under: (a) Rework to spec, (b) Repair via EOR procedure, (c) Scrap/Reject, or (d) Concession/Use-As-Is.

4

Verification & Closeout

Re-inspection, destructive/non-destructive testing, and formal closure signoff prior to releasing subsequent downstream hold points.

1. Protocol Mandate & Governance Architecture
Mandatory Governance

The 5-Stage Project Quality Gatekeeper Protocol (PQGP) provides a non-negotiable governance and quality verification mechanism tailored for high-complexity building construction (commercial, residential, institutional, and mixed-use). Construction projects face substantial latent risks across structural integrity, architectural fit-out, statutory compliance, MEP integration, and commercial liability.

This protocol operates on the principle of Hard Gating: No project stage may mobilize, procure substantial works, or execute physical site installations until the designated Gatekeeper Committee conducts a formal review and issues a verified Gate Certificate.

3. Detailed Stage Protocols & Execution Criteria
Technical Execution Criteria
GATE 1 Pre-Construction, Constructability & Design Baseline
Stage 1 Protocols
  • BIM Coordination: Zero 'Hard Clashes' between structural load-bearing elements and gravity drainage/HVAC mains (Navisworks Clash Log certified).
📐 BIM Coordination: The process of combining 3D digital blueprints from the architect, structural engineer, and mechanical/plumbing trades into one master computer model to make sure everything fits together.
⚡ Zero 'Hard Clashes': No two physical solid objects occupy the exact same space in the computer model (for example, a large pipe isn't drawn directly through the middle of a solid steel column).
🏗️ Structural Load-Bearing Elements: The critical "bones" that hold the building up and keep it standing—such as concrete columns, load-bearing walls, structural slabs, and steel beams.
🚰 Gravity Drainage / HVAC Mains: The largest and most rigid utilities in a building:
  • Gravity drainage: Waste and sewer pipes that must slant downward at a strict angle so wastewater flows naturally. Because they need a constant slope, you cannot easily bend them around obstacles on-site.
  • HVAC mains: The largest central air ducts and chilled water pipes that heat, cool, and ventilate the building.
  • Geotechnical Validation: Bearing capacity and soil stabilization confirmed against foundation structural design parameters.
  • Permit Approvals: Barangay Clearance, environmental clearance, and city municipal building permits fully acquired.
  • Quality Baseline: Project schedule, 3D Construction Methodologies, Material Submittal Schedule (MSS), and Site Safety/Excavation Shoring Plans.
GATE 2 Substructure, Groundworks & Foundation Release
Stage 2 Protocols
  • Pile Integrity & Load Tests: Geotechnical Analysis recommended by third party (BIP Steadfast).
  • Sub-Slab Barrier: Continuous blindside waterproofing inspection with spark testing / flood testing verified prior to slab casting.
  • Raft Mass Concreting: Thermal monitoring log for mass concrete pours (maximum core-to-surface temperature differential ≤ 20°C).

💧 Concrete Moisture Management, Physical Protection & Early Joint Cutting

💧 Moisture Management (Curing)
  • Start Immediately: Begin curing as soon as the concrete surface is hard enough to resist damage from the method used (usually right after the bleed water evaporates and final finishing is complete).
  • Wet Methods: Keep the surface constantly wet using continuous water misting/sprinkling, ponding (for flat slabs), or covering with wet burlap/hessian blankets covered by polyethylene plastic sheeting to prevent evaporation.
  • Chemical Curing Compounds: Spray an approved liquid membrane-forming curing compound that seals moisture inside the slab if wet curing is impractical.
  • Duration: Maintain moisture continuously for a minimum of 7 days (or at least 3 days for high-early-strength concrete; 14 days for slow-curing/mass pours).
🛡️ Physical & Load Protection
  • Foot Traffic: Keep all foot traffic off the slab for at least 24 to 48 hours.
  • Heavy Equipment & Vehicles: Prohibit construction vehicles, machinery, and concentrated material loads for at least 7 to 28 days (until verified by test cylinder breaks).
  • Formwork Striking Times: Do not strip side forms, vertical supports, or reshores prematurely; adhere to structural engineering schedules based on field-cured compressive strength.
✂️ Early Contraction Joint Cutting
  • Saw-cut control/contraction joints early—typically within 6 to 18 hours after pouring in warm weather (or 12 to 24 hours in cool weather)—before internal shrinkage stresses create random diagonal cracking.
  • Cut joints to a depth of 1/4 the slab thickness.

GATE 3 Superstructure Framing & Building Envelope Integrity
Stage 3 Protocols
  • Framing & Tolerances: Laser scanning for slab deflection and verticality of columns/cores within ACI 117 / BS 5606 tolerances.
  • Envelope Field Tests: AAMA 501.2 hose testing and ASTM E1105 field water penetration testing for unitized curtain wall assemblies.
  • Roof Waterproofing: 48-hour hydrostatic flood test or electronic vector mapping (EVM) for green roofs and podium slabs.
  • Structural Sign-Off: Concrete cylinder compressive logs (28-day), NDT steel weld testing, and facade anchor pull-out test reports.
GATE 4 MEP Rough-in, Life Safety & Architectural Fit-Out
Stage 4 Protocols
  • Pressure & Containment: Hydrostatic pressure testing of chilled water, domestic water, and sprinkler piping held at 150 psi for 24h with zero loss.
  • Firestopping Certification: 100% visual and destructive audit of UL-listed firestop penetration seals across floor/wall fire separations.
  • First-Off Mockups: Approved benchmark mockups for drywall finishes, tiling, joinery, and sanitary fixtures establishing tolerance baselines.
  • Above-Ceiling Sign-Off: Formal multi-disciplinary sign-off (HVAC, Electrical, Plumbing, Fire Alarm) prior to closing drywall ceilings.
GATE 5 Integrated Commissioning, Handover & Practical Completion
Stage 5 Protocols
  • Black Building Test: Total main utility power disconnection to prove emergency generator start, ATS transfer, and life-safety operation ≤ 10s.
  • HVAC TAB Balancing: Airflow balance report, chilled water flow balancing, thermal comfort audit, and IAQ verification.
  • Statutory Sign-Off: Civil Defense smoke extraction, stair pressurization, and emergency voice alarm communication (EVAC) sign-off.
  • Closeout Assets: Final Certificate of Occupancy (C of O), As-Built BIM LOD 500 / COBie data, and O&M warranty dossiers.

5. Quality Gate Approval & Certification Workflow
Committee Protocol

A Gate Review Meeting is convened at least 15 working days prior to target phase completion. The Project Quality Manager presents the Gate Review Dossier containing all ITP inspection records, lab test reports, as-built redlines, and third-party certifications. Sign-off requires unanimous assent from the voting committee:
